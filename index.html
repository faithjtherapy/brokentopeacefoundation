01 / Our mission

Every young woman deserves a place to belong.

Our mission is to provide housing services for youth in care, ages 12 to 19. We believe a safe home starts with people who show up, listen, and keep showing up.

We are building toward a small group home for girls ages 12 to 16, with a transitional living program planned for year two. The goal is to help young women move toward independence with trusted relationships beside them.

Fewer beds. Longer relationships.
Real preparation for adulthood.

02 / The program we’re building

Two stages of growing.
One steady circle of care.

Our planned model connects a small group home with a next step toward independent living, so growing older can mean moving forward with support.

Planned phase 1 · Year 1

01
Ages 12 to 16

A home to settle into.

A planned group home for two to four girls, subject to DCFS licensing. A small setting centered on everyday stability, school continuity, and care that responds to each young person.

  • 24/7 qualified staff coverage
  • Coordination with schools and DCFS caseworkers
  • Health, mental health, and substance-use support
  • Sexual health and pregnancy support when needed

Planned phase 2 · Year 2

02
Ages 17 to 19

Space to step forward.

A planned transitional living placement for young women growing beyond the group home. Semi-independent living with practical preparation and continued clinical support.

  • Continuity with trusted staff and a care team
  • Everyday life skills and financial literacy
  • Job readiness and employment planning
  • College and vocational pathways

Our goal: the same trusted relationships, through the next chapter.

Both phases are in development. Timing depends on licensing, funding, and a suitable home.

03 / Our services

Care that reaches beyond the home.

Programs we plan to offer alongside our residential work, open to schools, congregations, and community partners.

01

Training & education

Sessions for staff, church members, and community partners on youth development, trauma-informed practice, and supporting young people in care.

02

Mind over matter

Anxiety and depression workshops, plus trauma and stress management groups, led by qualified clinicians.

03

Essential care kits

Personal care and hygiene essentials distributed to students who may face barriers accessing these necessities.

04 / Our approach

Care is in the everyday.

The way we plan to work is as important as the home we hope to open.

01

Relationship first

A small resident count creates room for connection. Our model puts consistent, trusted adults at the center of care.

02

Growing together

The planned transition from ages 12 through 19 is built around continuity, with support that grows as young women do.

03

Thoughtful care

Trauma-informed practice guides our plans, with mental health and substance-use support woven into everyday care.

04

Chicago rooted

We’re exploring local housing and funding opportunities to build a home connected to the community around it.

06 / Resources

Help you can reach right now.

If you or someone you love needs support today, start here. These are independent services, separate from Broken to Peace.

01

988 Suicide & Crisis Lifeline

Call or text 988 to reach a trained counselor, 24 hours a day. Free and confidential. Learn what to expect ↗

02

Crisis Text Line

Text HOME to 741741 to reach a crisis counselor by text, any time of day or night. Visit Crisis Text Line ↗

03

Wellness & wellbeing

Everyday support for mental health and self-care: SAMHSA National Helpline, The Trevor Project for LGBTQ+ youth, and NAMI for family support.

In an emergency, call 911. The resources above are provided for information only. Broken to Peace does not operate a crisis line and cannot respond to urgent mental health needs.
